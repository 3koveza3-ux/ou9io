1. **TushBaby's size** — $50M brand with strong brand loyalty. You're not killing them; you're carving off their overflow demand. Stay at $59–65, don't try to out-premium them at $80+.

# BRAND NAME: "NESTLI STORE" — VERDICT

## ⚠️ Conditional — Needs Adjustment

**What I found live:**
1. **nestlibaby.com** already exists and is an active baby brand
2. **nestlybaby.com** also exists (active)
3. **Nestlé** (the global food corporation) has extensive baby product lines (Gerber, NAN formula) and is aggressive on trademark enforcement globally

**The problem:** "Nestli" is close enough to "Nestlé" that you could receive a cease-and-desist in any EU or Swiss jurisdiction. In the US it's less likely but not impossible, especially if you advertise in markets where Nestlé baby products are prominent.

**The second problem:** "Store" in a brand name reads generic and drops trust. "Nestli Store" sounds like a marketplace, not a brand. TushBaby is "TushBaby," not "TushBaby Store." Momcozy is "Momcozy," not "Momcozy Store."

## RECOMMENDATION:

**Option A (Keep "Nestli" feel):**  
— Drop "Store." Just call it **"Nestli"** and use the suffix on products (NestliCarry, NestliRest).  
— First check nestli.com availability and trademark clearance in your target markets before committing.

**Option B (Safer alternatives):**
- **NestWell™** — "Nest" (home/nurturing) + "Well" (wellness). Clean, not trademark-adjacent. Likely available.
- **NestCo™** — Short, memorable, brand-scalable.
- **Carrynest™** — Product-specific but fits the carrier as hero.

**My pick if you want to stay close to the original feel: Use "Nestli" as the brand, drop "Store," and file a trademark in your home market before scaling ads. Check USPTO (US), EUIPO (EU), and CIPO (Canada) before launch.**
